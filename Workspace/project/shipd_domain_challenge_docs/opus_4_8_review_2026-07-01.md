## (c) Per-idea detailed assessments

### A1 — Error-Typed QE for Code-Mixed Support MT — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium-high)
Fit is solid: this is a genuine regression+multilabel fine-tuning task on a synthetic register no pretrained QE model has seen, and the worst-community term is a real semantic axis. The nearest neighbor (WMT QE / TransQuest / OpenKiwi) is named honestly and the delta (structured multi-type error attribution + code-mixed register + worst-group) is real; RECOMBINED 5–6 is a fair estimate.
**The one concern that matters:** the difficulty cap. The scored heads are QE score MAE + error-type F1 — both are *learnable to near-ceiling* once you have 18k labeled pairs from a deterministic rule+noise MT simulator, because the injected error families are generator-known. If the error signature is even mildly regular, a fine-tuned encoder saturates and there is no irreducible-loss floor (this is exactly the score-saturation trap: the cap is "a suboptimal baseline," not information loss). **Fix:** bake in genuine label ambiguity — e.g. a fraction of rows where two error families are truly indistinguishable from surface, and make the ground-truth `quality_score` a noisy human-like judgment with irreducible variance, so oracle < 1.0 on the score head. Verify a strong fine-tune caps well under 0.7 before building.

### A2 — Incident Narrative Chronology + Causal-Link Reconstruction — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
This is the strongest of the "reconstruct hidden order/causality" ideas because it moved off structured logs (spent, and the source of the Ops-Log ~0.94 failure) onto genuine first-person flashback narrative, and it scores a *global permutation + causal graph* jointly rather than pairwise labels. TempEval/RED/CaTeRS are named; the delta is real.
**Concern:** the draft leans on "first-stated order is wrong ~60% of the time" as the difficulty. That is a *distributional* trick, not irreducible — a model that learns the generator's flashback grammar can invert it perfectly, and with only 5 style families and one held out, the held-out family is a thin moat. Also, the causal-edge head is only as hard as the candidate-pair list makes it: if candidate pairs are few, edge-F1 is near-trivially high. **Fix:** ensure some narratives are *genuinely order-ambiguous* (two events with no linguistic time-anchor) so the permutation head has an irreducible-loss ceiling, and widen the causal candidate set. Confirm oracle < 1.0.

### A3 — Cross-Lingual Risk-Signal Embedding Alignment — APPROVE (confidence: high)
**One of my top picks.** I verified the in-house-neighbor claim directly: `NLP.md`'s "Representation Learning for Compositional Generalization from Survey Reports" is the only grader-side linear-probe / submit-embeddings task, and it is *monolingual* with a compositional (crystal, environment) holdout. A3's holdout axis is cross-**language** transfer with a worst-language probe term — a genuinely different robustness property, and `NLP.md` contains zero cross-lingual/worst-language scoring (grep-confirmed). The "submit representations, no labels exist" design structurally forecloses the label-fitting shortcut, and the grader's rank/variance check on submitted embeddings blocks one-hot smuggling (the crystal task already established this guard, so it is a proven pattern). Worst-language minimum is a legitimate *irreducible* cap: closing the cross-lingual gap on a synthetic register genuinely requires training, and off-the-shelf multilingual encoders provably cluster by surface language first.
**Minor:** the D-range 8–256 is fine; keep the probe protocol (StandardScaler on train only, then frozen linear probe) identical to the accepted crystal task for validator familiarity. No blocking issues.

### A4 — Argument-Scheme Graph + Counterfactual Warrant Sensitivity — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
The counterfactual-per-edge head ("does the claim survive if this evidence is struck") is a fresh mechanism (the saturated-patterns catalog lists "counterfactual survives-if-removed" as newly introduced in this very batch, so it is not yet spent), and the confidence-vs-weight decoupling trap is a direct, well-motivated anti-shortcut. Project Debater / AraucariaDB / IAM are named honestly.
**Concern:** buildability of a *faithful* counterfactual label. "claim_weakens vs claim_collapses" is a 3-way judgment that must be generated deterministically from the argument-scheme template — if the mapping from warrant-strength to the 3 classes is a fixed function of generator state, a fine-tuned model can recover it exactly and the head saturates; if it is fuzzy, the labels risk being arbitrary (a `prev_reviews` rejection pattern: "creator-defined labels with no real workflow"). **Fix:** ground the 3-class boundary in a *stated, defensible* rule (e.g. collapse iff removing the evidence leaves the claim with zero load-bearing support; weakens iff it drops below a support-count threshold) and expose that this is derived from argument structure, not a subjective call. Also confirm the multi-evidence interaction cases (two weak evidences jointly sufficient) create irreducible ambiguity for the cap.

### A5 — Peer-Review Rebuttal Point Alignment under Hidden Per-Venue Rubric — CUT (confidence: high)
The draft itself flags this as its first cut candidate, and I agree — more strongly than the draft. Three reasons: (1) **Novelty:** it sits directly on DISAPERE (a *named, published* review-rebuttal alignment dataset with an alignment-category label schema). Per-sub-point alignment + a per-venue term is a thin delta over "we annotate rebuttal alignment," and the automated novelty checker (which the Inverse-Shadow log shows returning 4/10 even after real reframing near a named neighbor) is likely to cap this below the 5/10 gate. (2) **Mechanism saturation:** it combines *two* already-heavily-used hooks — "hidden per-venue convention learned from examples" (the single most overused template per the saturated-patterns catalog) AND the tone-vs-substance confidence trap (also used in A4). It reads as a 16th "induce a hidden institutional convention" instance. (3) **Gradeability:** "directly_addressed vs partially_addressed vs deflected" is an inherently subjective 4-way label; making it deterministic enough for `grade.py==1.0` on perfect labels risks the arbitrary-label rejection. Drop it; A4 already carries the tone-vs-substance idea in a fresher frame.

### B1 — Grade-2 Braille Back-Translation Error Localization — APPROVE (confidence: medium-high)
**A quietly strong pick.** Braille-ASCII has near-zero pretraining representation, so the training-beats-prompting gap is real and hard to fake; the "same cell has 3–6 valid context-dependent expansions, so a lookup table provably fails" is a *legitimate irreducible* difficulty grounded in the actual grade-2 ruleset, not a noise trick. The public-domain contraction ruleset makes the generator honest and CC0-clean. `has_error`/`error_type`/`error_span` is a clean, strictly gradeable schema. External collision risk is genuinely low (liblouis QA tooling is the nearest neighbor; no Kaggle/HF benchmark owns this).
**Only caveat (not blocking):** confirm real practitioner appetite (the draft says so) and make sure the span-IoU head can't be gamed by always predicting a wide span — cap span width or score IoU strictly. This is a build-priority-worthy idea precisely because its difficulty is structural.

### B2 — ATC Readback Error Detection + Span Localization — APPROVE (confidence: high)
Strong, well-grounded, buildable. ICAO numeral phraseology ("tree/fife/niner") + callsign-confusability is a narrow register that fine-tuning genuinely helps with; the worst-error-type-recall term correctly prevents coasting on easy digit substitutions. The killed-shortcut list is thoughtful — critically, it explicitly blocks the naive edit-distance shortcut by including *correct paraphrase-style readbacks that must NOT be flagged*, which is exactly the kind of adversarial cue-label decoupling that defeated the Ops-Log surface shortcut. ATCO2/ATCOSIM named; text-only + synthetic sidesteps their audio-licensing. RECOMBINED is fair.
**Watch item, not a revision:** the 6-class error head skews to "none" ~40% — make sure macro-F1 (not accuracy) is the head so the "none" majority can't carry the score, and that callsign_confusion has enough test count per dialect group to keep the worst-group term stable (the `MIN_GROUP_TEST>=20` guard from prev_reviews applies).

### B3 — Patent Claim Dependency + Indefiniteness Flagging — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
The genuinely novel bit is the behavioral inversion: prompted LLMs "helpfully fix" apparent typos, but the correct legal behavior is to *flag* a broken antecedent as indefiniteness — a real, well-motivated failure mode. The full-tree-validity bonus rewards global coherence. US Patent Phrase-to-Phrase (Kaggle) is named; the task is different in kind.
**Concern:** the dependency-tree head is largely *parseable* ("claim N depends on the number stated in its reference phrase") — the draft's own baseline (reference-phrase regex) will already score high on the ~65% of non-adversarial rows, so the score can be carried by an easy head. The real difficulty is concentrated in the ~10% indefiniteness-trap rows. **Fix:** rebalance weights toward the indefiniteness-flag F1 and the trap subset, and/or make more references genuinely ambiguous ("claim 1 or 2" branching) so the parse head itself has irreducible error. Otherwise a shallow parser + "never flag" scores dangerously high (the same shape as the Marble-Run read-off problem).

### B4 — VHF Maritime Transcript Standardization — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
Clean under-benchmarked vertical, good distress-class 2x weighting (missing a distress is correctly penalized hardest), and the "mayday appears in non-distress drill exchanges" trap blocks the keyword trigger. Low collision risk.
**Concern:** the `intent_summary` head is scored by chrF++ against a *single canonical SMCP reference phrase*. Free-text generation scored against one reference is the weakest part — it is both gameable (copy common SMCP boilerplate) and noisy (many valid paraphrases score low). **Fix:** either constrain intent_summary to a slot-filled canonical template (so exact-match/slot-F1 is possible) or down-weight it and lean on the closed-vocabulary message_type/urgency heads, which are strictly gradeable. As drafted, chrF++ against one reference risks a soft, saturating score.

### B5 — Museum Conservation Condition-Report Structuring — CUT (confidence: medium-high)
This is the weakest Fine-Tuning idea and I'd drop it. It is, mechanically, object-type-conditioned multilabel term extraction with a validity mask — i.e. "classify this note into a controlled vocabulary, conditioned on a visible type column." That is close to the `prompt_final_NLP` weak-reject line ("topic classification / classify this text without a new hard contract") and to the many domain-shorthand-extraction tasks already in the catalog. The object-type-validity rule ("craquelure only for paintings") is a *lookup constraint a prompted LLM largely already knows or can be told*, so the training-beats-prompting gap is weak — a zero-shot prompt with the vocabulary and validity table in-context would likely do well, violating hard constraint #2. Difficulty is Easy-Medium by the draft's own admission. Cut in favor of B1/B2/B4, which have real learned-notation depth.

### C1 — Proof Gap-Fill with Verifier-Checked Multi-Valid Completions — APPROVE (confidence: medium-high)
**A top pick despite the build cost.** The "any verifier-valid completion scores 1.0" grading is a genuinely rare and *correct* paradigm for this platform (most challenges force a single reference string; this rewards real generalization and eliminates the reference-paraphrase noise that hurts B4/C4). A bespoke shallow calculus kills pretraining leakage cleanly, and the held-out rule-family-combination split tests real rule-system induction. miniF2F/GPT-f named; the "shallow bespoke calculus to isolate seq-repair from deep math" delta is legitimate.
**The real cost (the draft flags it honestly):** a deterministic symbolic verifier must ship *inside* `grade.py` and be bulletproof — it must reject invalid completions, be bounded in runtime (apply the JSON/parse-length and element caps from the Inverse-Shadow lesson), and never crash on adversarial input. Build effort is above average. **Recommendation:** approve, but scope the calculus deliberately small (≤20 rules) and write the verifier + its adversarial tests first. The graph-edit-distance partial-credit fallback is good and prevents an all-or-nothing degenerate score.

### C2 — Cross-Sentence KG-Triple from Assembly Manuals — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
The cross-sentence coreference difficulty ("nearest-noun is wrong ~40% of the time; correct antecedent is 2+ sentences back") is real and fine-tuning-amenable, and the ordered-sequence + rare-relation-recall + pronoun-resolution decomposition prevents coasting on common relations. WebNLG/DocRED named; the ordered, closed-procedural-vocabulary delta is real.
**Concern:** overlaps conceptually with E2 (also "assembly instruction text → dependency graph, per-family convention, relabeling-tolerant Jaccard"). Two assembly-graph ideas in one batch is redundant — and E2 is the weaker of the two (see E2). Keep C2, cut E2. **Revision:** ensure the relabeling-tolerant matching cannot be exploited by emitting a generic high-frequency triple set; the rare-relation-recall term partly guards this but verify the empty/degenerate baseline scores low.

### C3 — Meeting Action-Items + Supersession/Hedge-Culture — CUT (confidence: medium-high)
The draft flags it as a cut candidate; I confirm the cut. (1) **"GPT already does this":** the draft's own novelty note admits the vertical "risks reading as something GPT already does well" — meeting action-item extraction is squarely in commercial-tool territory (Otter/Fireflies) and AMI/QMSum. The supersession-chain + hedge-culture additions are real but incremental, and a strong prompted LLM plausibly handles a large fraction zero-shot, threatening hard constraint #2. (2) **Mechanism:** "hidden per-culture-family pragmatics learned from examples" is again the overused hidden-convention template. (3) **Gradeability:** action-item *set F1 with free-text task strings* + supersession edges + ECE calibration is a heavy, partly-subjective grader; getting perfect labels to score exactly 1.0 deterministically is hard when "task" is free text. Cut.

### C4 — Historical Ledger Abbreviation Expansion — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
Position-dependent abbreviation disambiguation ("name-position Jn. vs date-position Jn.") is a real ByT5-flavored character-level task, and the ambiguous-abbreviation subset is a legitimate irreducible-difficulty axis (a static dictionary provably cannot resolve it). The in-house neighbor (Rosetta Egyptian) is correctly distinguished — no translation step, known-abbreviation disambiguation vs. unknown-damage restoration. Low collision.
**Concern:** two of the heads (`expanded_text` char-edit-similarity, and structured field exact-match) risk being *easy* — most abbreviations are unambiguous, so a model gets high char-similarity cheaply, and the score is carried by the easy 80%. **Fix:** weight the ambiguous-abbreviation subset more heavily (it's only 0.10 now) and confirm the OCR-noise injection creates genuine irreducible error so `expanded_text` oracle < 1.0. Also confirm real practitioner framing (genealogy transcription QA is plausible; a 15-min sanity check is cheap insurance).

### C5 — Recipe-to-Robot-Command with Implicit-Step Insertion — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
The implicit-command-inference core (predict steps with *no textual mention*, e.g. PREHEAT before BAKE) is the most interesting seq2seq mechanism in section C, and correctly weighting the implicit-command F1 above the explicit term (0.40 vs 0.35) directly targets precondition reasoning over extraction. The held-out cuisine-style split (styles differ in *which* steps they leave implicit) is a real generalization axis.
**Concern (the draft flags it honestly):** ALFRED/PlanBench vertical overlap and RECOMBINED-at-best. The differentiator (implicit-step insertion as dominant metric, text-only) is real but the novelty checker may still cap it. **Also:** the implicit steps are generated by a *fixed precondition planner* — if the precondition rules are deterministic and the held-out style shares the same physics, a model that learns the planner recovers implicit steps exactly and the head saturates. The moat is entirely the held-out style's *different implicit-step convention*; make sure that difference is substantive (not cosmetic) or the cap fails. Approve conditional on demonstrating the held-out-style gap is large.

### D1 — Fixed-Tokenizer BPC + Compositional Register Transfer — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium-high)
**My #1 build pick, with a mandatory Phase-2 revision on submission format.** The Pre-Training domain is confirmed empty (I verified `Pre-Training.md`: 0 accepted examples), so anything credible here is high-value. I also verified the mechanism claim: the nearest platform LM-training task, "Byte-Level LLM Pretraining: The MIPS32 Autopsy," scores a *supervised seq2seq target* (per-character accuracy on the C-library-call sequence), NOT direct next-token cross-entropy/BPC on held-out text. So **D1's direct bits-per-character under a grader-fixed tokenizer, skill-scored against a unigram reference, is genuinely novel on this platform** — and it is the cleanest possible demonstration of "training beats prompting," which is the entire thesis of this wave. The compositional-register-mixture holdout is a real irreducible generalization axis (and cross-entropy has a genuine irreducible floor — this is a *correct* cap, not a noise trick). The fixed grader tokenizer correctly forecloses the "tiny custom vocab compresses trivially" exploit.
**The blocking revision — submission format.** A top-20-tokens+top-20-probabilities-per-position CSV over a ~2MB eval stream is the single biggest risk in the whole batch. It could be enormous, it is unusual for this platform's graders, and a naive proper-scoring implementation over a truncated top-20 distribution needs care to stay strictly proper (the "other" bucket handling must be well-defined). **Fix before build:** (a) cap the number of scored positions hard (sample a few thousand positions, not the whole 2MB stream), (b) define the truncated-distribution scoring rigorously (the remaining mass → single "other" token with a floor probability, and document it), (c) bound the CSV size and reject oversize submissions. If the format proves impractical, fall back to scoring log-prob of the *true* token only (submitter emits one probability per scored position for the known-to-grader true token) — still a valid BPC skill score, far smaller submission. Do the format spike first.

### D2 — Surprisal-Based Anomaly Localization from a From-Scratch Domain LM — APPROVE (confidence: medium-high)
Strong and mechanistically clean. The negation-flip perturbation type is the star: "the valve was closed" vs "the valve was not closed" are *equally grammatical*, so a generic fluency/grammaticality checker provably cannot catch it — only a model that learned genuine domain co-occurrence statistics can, which is a real irreducible gap and a correct hard-to-fake cap. Isolating negation_flip recall as its own scored term is exactly right. BLiMP named; the delta (solver must pretrain their own domain model; domain-semantic not syntactic; localization+classification not pairwise preference) is real and substantive.
**Minor:** the submission (`anomalous_sentence_index`, `perturbation_type`) is clean and strictly gradeable — a welcome contrast to D1's format. Confirm the held-out equipment_subdomain has enough test docs for the worst-group term, and that term-substitution anomalies are subtle enough (swap to a plausible-but-wrong component) that surface-frequency detection fails. No blocking issues.

### D3 — Dual-Orthography Zero-Shot Cross-Script Transfer — APPROVE (confidence: medium-high)
Excellent fit and a genuinely irreducible difficulty: Script B shares *zero surface tokens* with Script A by construction, so zero-shot transfer *requires* the solver to build a script-invariant representation from the small bridge set — this cannot be prompted away and is the training-beats-prompting thesis in pure form. The script-invariance consistency term (agreement across the two renderings of the same held-out sentence) is a smart guard against "accidentally accurate on B." The saturated-patterns catalog lists "dual-orthography cross-script transfer" as newly introduced this batch, so it is fresh. XTREME named; the same-language-two-scripts vs. different-languages delta is real.
**Watch:** the label task (topic/intent classification) is itself easy; the entire difficulty is the transfer, so make sure Script-A accuracy is near-ceiling for a trained model (sanity term) while Script-B stays the binding constraint. Keep the bridge set genuinely small (a few thousand) — if it's too large it becomes a full parallel corpus and the challenge collapses. Approve.

### D4 — Cross-Format Config-Schema Pretraining with Held-Out Dialect — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
Good "learn semantics abstracted from surface syntax" pretraining task; the held-out FOURTH dialect (never seen during pretraining) is a real format-zero-shot axis, and the invented/renamed key-space blocks public config-key lookup. Low collision.
**Concern:** the schema-consistency bonus is scored "per a lenient rubric" — lenient/subjective grading terms are a recurring rejection trigger and are hard to make deterministic (`grade.py==1.0` on perfect labels). **Fix:** drop the fuzzy rubric term or replace it with a deterministic type/plausibility check (predicted value is type-consistent with the surrounding key). Also, a generic LLM has *broad* real YAML/TOML/INI exposure — verify that the invented key-space and the 4th dialect are alien enough that zero-shot prompting genuinely fails (the draft argues this; confirm empirically in `_analyze.py`).

### E1 — Tone-Sandhi Surface-Form Prediction — APPROVE (confidence: high)
**Best of the From-Scratch section.** I verified it does not collide with the invented-vocab cloze entries (Mermaid/Sanskrit/dialect-MLP are cloze/next-token; this is surface-form sequence prediction). Crucially, its delta over the in-house "Neural Dialect Next-Token Prediction" is real and substantive: that task adapts to an *arbitrary random-MLP function* with zero structure; tone sandhi has genuine *learnable phonological regularity* (rule types recur across dialects with different parameters/directions), so the large unlabeled corpus provably helps and few-shot dialect adaptation is meaningful. The reversed-direction dialects block a single global lookup table (real irreducible difficulty for the worst-dialect term). Clean, strictly gradeable per-syllable + whole-sequence metric. Low collision. Approve as-is.

### E2 — Assembly Dependency-Graph from Kit Text — CUT (confidence: medium-high)
Cut for **redundancy and mechanism overlap**, not because it's bad in isolation. It is the same core as C2 (assembly-instruction text → dependency graph, per-family convention induction, relabeling-tolerant typed-Jaccard scoring). The relabeling-tolerant Jaccard trick is explicitly borrowed from the accepted "UAV Mesh Grammar Decompilation" (the draft admits this), and the "family-conditioned attachment-direction reversal" is a thinner version of the same convention-induction idea that pervades the batch. Between C2 (natural-ish prose, cross-sentence coreference, rare-relation term) and E2 (invented ~200-token vocabulary, direction-reversal), C2 is the richer task and better fits its NLP lane. Two assembly-graph challenges dilute the batch; keep C2.

### E3 — Non-Manual-Marker Scope over Sign-Gloss Notation — APPROVE (confidence: medium-high)
Genuinely fresh accessibility-technology framing with no known Kaggle/HF neighbor, and using a *wholly constructed* gloss notation cleanly sidesteps the real cultural/community-data sensitivities around actual sign-language corpora (a thoughtful, reviewer-friendly choice). The nested/overlapping-span subset is a real structural difficulty that flat BIO-tagging provably cannot handle — isolating it as its own term is correct, and it's an irreducible-by-design axis. Note the draft correctly hides `construction_family` at test time to prevent a family-lookup shortcut. Approve; strong low-collision addition.

### E4 — Numeral-Classifier Selection under Semantic-Domain Holdout + Abstention — APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS (confidence: medium)
The honest-abstention mechanism (`AMBIGUOUS` when a noun genuinely takes 2+ classifiers by register) is a real linguistic property modeled correctly rather than forced into a single label — exactly what `prompt_final_NLP` constraint #6 asks for, and the saturated-patterns catalog lists this honest-multi-valid-abstention as freshly introduced. The held-out-semantic-domain split tests analogical generalization of the assignment *principle*, not memorization. Low collision.
**Concern:** two risks. (1) It leans toward classification-with-abstention, which is on the lighter side; the difficulty rests entirely on the held-out domain and the calibration term — make sure the held-out domain is far enough from training that a dictionary provably fails (the draft argues this). (2) The `AMBIGUOUS` F1 and the ECE calibration term must be strictly deterministic and the ambiguity ground truth non-arbitrary. **Fix:** ensure `ambiguity_ground_truth` derives from a stated rule (noun's semantic features map to ≥2 classifier prototypes above a similarity threshold), and confirm the always-confident-guess baseline is clearly beaten by the calibration/abstention terms.

## Cross-cutting concerns (systemic across the batch)

1. **Distribution-trick caps vs. irreducible-loss caps (the score-saturation lesson, applied hard).** A recurring pattern: many ideas rest their difficulty on a *learnable distributional quirk* of a deterministic generator — "first-stated order wrong 60% of the time" (A2), fixed error families from a rule+noise MT sim (A1), a deterministic precondition planner (C5), a fixed argument-scheme→3-class map (A4), position-dependent-but-deterministic expansions (C4). A model with thousands of labeled pairs learns the generator, the head saturates near 1.0, and the "cap" evaporates — exactly the failure mode of the score-saturation lesson and the Marble-Run 0.90 read-off. The ideas that *don't* have this problem cap on **genuine irreducible information loss**: D2's negation-flip (both grammatical — surface can't decide), D3's zero-shared-tokens transfer, D1's cross-entropy floor, A3's cross-lingual gap, B1's provably-multi-valued cells, E4's honest ambiguity. **Recommendation:** for every APPROVE-WITH-REVISIONS idea, the Phase-2 gate must be an *oracle-and-strong-solver sweep* (as done for Marble-Run and Inverse-Shadow) proving oracle=1.0 but strong fine-tune < ~0.7, with the cap coming from designed ambiguity, not an assumed-weak baseline.

2. **Free-text / single-reference / lenient-rubric grading heads are the gradeability weak spot.** B4's chrF++-vs-one-reference `intent_summary`, C3's free-text task strings, D4's "lenient rubric" schema-consistency bonus, and C4's char-edit-similarity all introduce soft, non-strict, or paraphrase-noisy scoring that fights the hard requirement that perfect labels score *exactly* 1.0 and invalid submissions score 0.0. The strongest ideas score only closed-vocabulary / structured / verifier-checked / exact-match heads (B1, B2, D2, D3, C1's verifier). **Recommendation:** wherever a free-text head appears, either constrain it to slots/canonical templates or down-weight it below the closed-form heads; never let a soft head be the dominant score.

3. **The "hidden per-X convention learned from examples" template is still over-represented, and clusters the batch's novelty risk.** A5, C3 (and to a lesser degree A4, B5, C5) all reduce to "induce a hidden institutional/cultural/style convention from labeled examples and classify its consequence" — the single most saturated template in this project's history per the catalog. The draft avoided the *exact 15 prior instances* but re-derived the same *shape* several times. The two CUTs most driven by this (A5, C3) plus B5 remove the worst offenders; the survivors (A4, C5) carry a fresher dominant mechanism (counterfactual survival; implicit-step insertion) and are salvageable. **Recommendation:** when shortlisting, prefer the ideas whose difficulty is a *mechanism* (BPC, verifier-validity, cross-script alignment, surprisal, sandhi phonology) over those whose difficulty is a *hidden convention*, and run the repo's dispatched novelty pre-vet on any hidden-convention survivor before committing.

*(Minor recurring reminders that apply to all builds, per LATEST.md / prev_reviews: keep min=0.0 and lift the sample to ≥~0.12 via label priors; enforce unique ids, exact columns, JSON length + element caps on every structured head; strictly separate predicted-value parsers from answer parsers; group-split so train/test variants of one scenario stay on one side and every worst-group bucket clears ~20 test rows; never leak held-out structure, rule names, or noise rates into the participant-facing description.)*

### N1. Tokenizer-Fertility-Aware Continued Pretraining For A Morphologically-Fused Synthetic Language (Pre-Training)

**Real-world context:** When teams continue-pretrain a base model on a morphologically rich or agglutinative/fusional language, the base model's BPE tokenizer — trained mostly on analytic languages — over-fragments words into many subword pieces ("high fertility"), which wastes context and degrades modeling. A real, published production concern is whether a solver can adapt a model so that its *predictive quality per byte* improves on exactly the high-fertility, morphologically-fused spans where the base tokenizer struggles most, not just on easy analytic spans.

**What the solver sees:** `corpus.txt` (~35MB unlabeled synthetic corpus in a constructed *fusional* language where a single surface word packs 2–5 morphemes with non-concatenative fusion — vowel changes, portmanteau affixes — so morpheme boundaries are not segmentable by surface regex), and a fixed `eval_tokenizer.json` (the grader's canonical byte-level tokenizer, published for shared scoring).

**What they predict:** for each scored position in a private held-out stream (released as context windows, no targets), the submitter emits the log-probability their continued-pretrained model assigns to the **single true next token** under the canonical tokenizer: `id, logprob` (one float per position). (Deliberately *not* a top-k distribution — this keeps the submission tiny and the scoring strictly proper, directly addressing D1's format risk.)

**Intended solution:** continued-pretrain a small fixed base model (Pythia-160M, Qwen2.5-0.5B) on the corpus, then emit next-token log-probs on the eval stream projected onto the canonical tokenizer. A model that never trained on the corpus assigns far less mass to the correct token, *especially* on high-fertility fused spans.

**Evaluation metric:** 0.55 x bounded per-byte cross-entropy skill score (normalized bits-per-*character* against a train-only unigram reference; 0 = unigram, 1 = perfect) + 0.30 x **high-fertility-span skill score** (the same metric computed *only* over positions inside words the base tokenizer fragments into ≥4 pieces — the dominant, hardest, irreducible term isolating exactly where morphological modeling matters) + 0.15 x low-fertility-span skill score (sanity term). Min 0.0, max 1.0. Perfect prediction → 1.0; cross-entropy has a genuine irreducible floor so oracle < 1.0 is guaranteed.

**Why hard for current agents:** a prompted/zero-shot LLM has never seen this fused morphology, so its per-byte surprisal on high-fertility spans is provably worse than a model actually continue-pretrained on the corpus; and because bits-per-character has an irreducible floor, this cannot be gamed to 1.0. Reporting log-prob of the true token (not free generation) makes the "training beats prompting" gap directly and strictly measurable.

**Why humans/experts can still improve it:** engineers who know morphology-aware continued-pretraining (tokenizer extension / re-fitting a subword inventory to the fused morphology, replay to avoid drift, learning-rate schedules) can lift the high-fertility-span term well beyond a naive full-fine-tune.

**Shortcuts killed:** tiny degenerate custom vocabularies (blocked by the fixed grader tokenizer + per-*character* normalization, so compressing into few tokens gives no advantage), constant/uniform log-probs (scored at the skill floor), unigram-frequency baseline (it *is* the zero point), and copying corpus n-gram stats without learning fusion (the high-fertility term specifically punishes surface-segment approaches).

**Data source & license:** fully synthetic (Option A). A generator defines a fusional grammar + non-concatenative morphophonology, emits the corpus and a private eval stream, tags each eval position with its base-tokenizer fertility, and publishes the canonical tokenizer. CC0 1.0.

**Dataset scale:** ~35MB unlabeled corpus; private eval stream sampled to ~4,000 scored positions (bounded submission). Well within budget.

**Split plan:** high-fertility vs. low-fertility positions are the hidden grader groups; a secondary held-out subset uses morpheme *combinations* absent from training (compositional morphology holdout).

**Schema:** public = corpus.txt, eval_tokenizer.json, eval context windows (no targets). Train-only = nothing (unsupervised; true next tokens known only to grader). Hidden grader groups = fertility bucket (dominant) and unseen-morpheme-combination subset. Submission = id, logprob (one finite float per scored position).

**Baseline plan:** `_analyze.py` reports unigram-frequency baseline (the zero point), zero-shot base-model log-probs (no continued pretraining), a generic-web-pretrained model, and a naively-overfit (no replay) continued-pretraining run — showing the high-fertility term specifically separating real morphological adaptation from surface memorization.

**Nearest-neighbor novelty note:** neighbors are general LM perplexity evaluation and D1 (this batch's own fixed-tokenizer BPC idea). Delta from D1: D1's dominant axis is *register-mixture compositional transfer* with a top-20-distribution submission; N1's dominant axis is *tokenizer-fertility / morphological modeling* with a strictly-proper single-true-token-logprob submission (smaller, safer format). Also neighbors the accepted "Byte-Level MIPS32" (which scores a supervised call-sequence, not BPC). Low external collision; genuinely fresh combination.
**Opus self-assessment of novelty risk: LOW-MODERATE.** Shares the BPC-skill-score family with D1, so if D1 is also built the two must be clearly differentiated (fertility vs. register axis); build at most one of {D1, N1} unless the differentiation is enforced in schema and framing.

**Suggested platform metadata:** Difficulty Hard; GPU A10G (compact base) or H100.

### N2. Learned Detokenization: Reconstructing Word Boundaries And Casing From An Unsegmented Scriptio-Continua Domain Stream (Seq2Seq / Pre-Training-adjacent)

**Real-world context:** Many real pipelines must recover structure from text with *no* word boundaries — historical *scriptio continua* manuscripts, hashtag/URL segmentation, agglutinative OCR output, ASR lattices without spaces. Correctly re-segmenting an unspaced stream into words (and restoring casing/punctuation) requires a language model of the domain, because the same character run is ambiguously segmentable ("therapistfinder" → "the rapist finder" vs "therapist finder") and only distributional knowledge disambiguates.

**What the solver sees:** `stream` (an unsegmented, lowercase, punctuation-stripped character run of a synthetic technical-domain text — e.g. a constructed equipment-log dialect — 60–160 chars, produced by concatenating a true token sequence and deleting all boundary/case/punctuation information) and `domain_family` (an opaque code for the sub-domain register).

**What they predict:** `segmentation` (the reconstructed sequence of word tokens, i.e. where to insert boundaries) plus `casing` (per reconstructed token: lower / Cap / UPPER) and `boundary_punct` (per gap: none / period / comma). The dominant head is segmentation.

**Intended solution:** train a small character-level sequence model (ByT5-small, or a from-scratch char Transformer) on (stream, segmentation) pairs — equivalently a per-character boundary-tagging + casing model — learning the domain's lexicon and collocation statistics well enough to resolve genuinely ambiguous runs. Zero-shot prompting fails because the domain lexicon is invented (no pretraining prior on which character runs are valid domain words).

**Evaluation metric:** 0.50 x word-boundary F1 (over inserted boundary positions) + 0.25 x **ambiguous-run subset accuracy** (an isolated, held-out subset of runs that admit ≥2 valid segmentations under the domain lexicon — scored against the *contextually correct* one; this is the irreducible-difficulty axis, since some runs remain genuinely ambiguous even for an oracle, capping the score) + 0.15 x casing accuracy + 0.10 x punctuation-restoration F1. Min 0.0, max 1.0. Perfect → 1.0; the ambiguous subset guarantees oracle < 1.0.

**Why hard for current agents:** the domain lexicon and collocations are invented, so a prompted LLM cannot segment reliably (it has no prior on which unspaced runs are valid words here); and greedy longest-match / dictionary segmentation provably fails on the ambiguous subset, which requires *contextual* disambiguation learned from the corpus. Character-level modeling from the provided data is required.

**Why humans/experts can still improve it:** researchers in word-segmentation (Chinese/Thai segmentation, unsupervised morphology) can add better collocation features and beam-search decoding over the boundary lattice, improving the ambiguous-run term beyond a naive per-character tagger.

**Shortcuts killed:** greedy dictionary longest-match (fails on the ambiguous subset by construction), stream length / character-count heuristics, majority-gap ("insert boundary every k chars"), domain_family name lookup (opaque; lexicon must be learned), and casing-by-position heuristics (Cap is not always token-initial-after-period by design).

**Data source & license:** fully synthetic (Option A). A generator samples a true cased+punctuated token sequence from an invented domain grammar/lexicon, records ground-truth boundaries/casing/punctuation, then deletes all of it to form the stream; ambiguous runs are logged by checking the lexicon for alternative segmentations. CC0 1.0.

**Dataset scale:** 14,000 train / 3,500 test streams, 60–160 chars each. A few tens of MB.

**Split plan:** grouped by `domain_family` (4 registers with different lexicons/collocations) with 1 held out entirely; ambiguous-run subset is a hidden grader group.

**Schema:** public = stream_id, stream, domain_family. Train-only = segmentation, casing, boundary_punct. Hidden grader groups = domain_family (worst-group) and ambiguous-run subset (dominant term). Submission = stream_id, segmentation (space-joined tokens), casing (list), boundary_punct (list).

**Baseline plan:** `_analyze.py` reports greedy-longest-match dictionary segmentation (fails on ambiguous subset), fixed-interval boundary baseline, per-character-frequency tagger, majority/constant, and a zero-shot-prompt proxy — all weak on the ambiguous subset and the held-out family.

**Nearest-neighbor novelty note:** neighbors are word-segmentation research (Chinese Word Segmentation bakeoffs, hashtag segmentation) and detokenization/truecasing tasks. No platform challenge targets *joint* boundary + casing + punctuation reconstruction from an invented-lexicon scriptio-continua domain stream with an isolated ambiguous-run cap. Distinct from C4 (abbreviation *expansion* of a still-spaced text) — here the difficulty is *boundary recovery under genuine ambiguity*. Low collision.
**Opus self-assessment of novelty risk: LOW.** The invented-lexicon mechanism is used elsewhere, but scriptio-continua joint re-segmentation with an ambiguity cap is a fresh contract; the ambiguous-run isolation is the differentiator to foreground.

**Suggested platform metadata:** Difficulty Medium; GPU A10G.

### N3. Register-Steered Constrained Rewriting Under A Hidden Lexical-Substitution Norm With A Meaning-Preservation Guard (Fine-Tuning)

**Real-world context:** Controlled text simplification/style-transfer systems (plain-language mandates for legal/medical/government text, reading-level adaptation) must rewrite a passage into a target register **while preserving meaning exactly and obeying a fixed forbidden/preferred-term list** — and the hard, real failure mode is that models silently drop or invert a critical fact when forced to avoid a banned term. This is a genuine, deployed compliance-rewriting workflow (e.g. rewriting to a controlled vocabulary without changing the covered entitlement).

**What the solver sees:** `passage` (a synthetic source passage in a "formal" register, 40–90 words, containing 2–4 checkable factual assertions — quantities, entities, polarity), a fixed `forbidden_terms` and `preferred_map` (a public controlled vocabulary the rewrite must obey), and `register_family` (opaque code for the target simplification norm — how aggressively to substitute, which hedges to keep).

**What they predict:** `rewrite` (the register-transferred text) — scored on three coupled axes: (a) constraint compliance (no forbidden term; required substitutions applied), (b) **meaning preservation** (the checkable assertions must survive), (c) register match to the hidden norm.

**Intended solution:** fine-tune a small instruction/seq2seq LM (Flan-T5, Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B) on (passage, compliant_rewrite) pairs to learn the per-family substitution norm *and* to preserve the checkable facts under the substitution constraint. Zero-shot prompting fails the hidden per-family norm and — critically — a prompted model told only "avoid these terms" measurably drops/inverts facts when the banned term carried the fact.

**Evaluation metric:** 0.30 x constraint-compliance score (deterministic: forbidden-term absence + preferred-substitution presence) + **0.40 x meaning-preservation score** (deterministic: each source assertion is a checkable slot — quantity/entity/polarity — extracted from source by the generator; the grader verifies each survives in the rewrite via exact/normalized slot match; this is the dominant term and the anti-shortcut core) + 0.20 x worst-register-family score (min across 3 held-out families) + 0.10 x a **fluency-vs-faithfulness trap term**: rows where the *fluent* rewrite tempts a polarity flip (e.g. "not eligible" → banned word forces a rephrase that models often turn into "eligible") — scored as polarity accuracy on just those rows. Min 0.0, max 1.0.

**Why hard for current agents:** a prompted LLM optimizes fluency + surface constraint compliance and, under the forbidden-term pressure, *silently alters meaning* — exactly what the dominant meaning-preservation term and the polarity-flip trap punish. The per-family norm (which substitutions are expected, which hedges kept) is invented and must be learned from labeled pairs. Because meaning-preservation is checked against generator-known assertion slots, it is strictly gradeable and hard to fake.

**Why humans/experts can still improve it:** plain-language and controlled-authoring specialists know how to substitute a banned term *without* touching polarity or quantity, and can tune constrained decoding (lexically-constrained beam search) to keep faithfulness high — beyond a naive fine-tune.

**Shortcuts killed:** copy-the-source (fails constraint compliance — forbidden terms remain), delete-the-hard-sentence (fails meaning preservation — dropped assertion), aggressive-substitution-that-flips-polarity (punished by the trap term), passage length, register_family name lookup (opaque), and constant/template rewrites (fail per-family register match).

**Data source & license:** fully synthetic (Option A). A generator builds a source passage with logged checkable assertion slots, a per-family substitution norm, and a *compliant* reference rewrite that provably preserves every slot; trap rows are constructed where the required substitution sits on a polarity-bearing clause. CC0 1.0.

**Dataset scale:** 9,000 train / 2,200 test passages. A few tens of MB.

**Split plan:** grouped by `register_family` (4 norms) with 1 held out entirely; the polarity-flip trap subset is a hidden grader group.

**Schema:** public = passage_id, passage, forbidden_terms, preferred_map, register_family. Train-only = compliant reference rewrite + assertion slots (slots never shown at test). Hidden grader groups = register_family (worst-group) and polarity-flip trap subset. Submission = passage_id, rewrite (text).

**Baseline plan:** `_analyze.py` reports copy-source baseline (0 on compliance-affected rows), naive-substitution regex baseline (high compliance, *low* meaning-preservation — exposes the core gap), delete-hard-sentence baseline, majority/template rewrite, and a zero-shot-prompt proxy — with the meaning-preservation and trap terms specifically separating faithful rewriting from surface compliance.

**Nearest-neighbor novelty note:** neighbors are controlled text simplification (Newsela/ASSET), lexically-constrained generation, and style transfer. This repo's catalog lists two biomedical *text-simplification* entries — but those score simplification quality, not a *coupled constraint-compliance + deterministic meaning-preservation-slot + polarity-flip trap* contract. The dominant meaning-preservation-via-checkable-slots head is the differentiator (it makes a normally-soft generation task strictly gradeable). Moderate proximity to simplification; the enforceable faithfulness contract is the delta.
**Opus self-assessment of novelty risk: MODERATE.** Text simplification/style transfer is a crowded space and the repo already has two simplification entries; this must foreground the deterministic meaning-preservation-slot + polarity-flip contract to clear the gate, and rename target columns off "simplification." Recommend the dispatched novelty pre-vet before build.

**Suggested platform metadata:** Difficulty Medium-Hard; GPU H100.

### N4. Grapheme-To-Phoneme Transduction With Stress Assignment For A Constructed Language Under A Loanword-Stratum Holdout (From-Scratch / Seq2Seq)

**Real-world context:** Text-to-speech front-ends and pronunciation-lexicon builders need grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion, and the genuinely hard part is *stress/accent placement* and handling *loanword strata* — words borrowed from another source stratum follow different stress and phoneme-mapping rules than native vocabulary, and a G2P model that memorizes the native rule fails on the borrowed layer. This is a real, published low-resource TTS / computational-phonology workflow.

**What the solver sees:** `corpus.txt` (a large unlabeled grapheme corpus of a constructed language) plus `lexicon.csv` (labeled grapheme→phoneme+stress pairs for training), where each word carries a hidden native-vs-loanword stratum that governs its rules — but the stratum label is **not** given at test time.

**What they predict:** `phoneme_sequence` (the phonemic transcription in a closed phoneme inventory) **and** `stress_pattern` (per-syllable primary/secondary/none) for held-out words.

**Intended solution:** train a small character/sequence seq2seq model (ByT5-small or from-scratch char Transformer) on the lexicon (optionally leveraging the unlabeled corpus for representation), learning both the regular native G2P mapping and the systematically-different loanword-stratum mapping, and inferring stratum from surface graphotactics (since it isn't labeled at test). Zero-shot prompting fails: the language and its phoneme inventory are invented, and the stratum-conditioned rules must be induced from data.

**Evaluation metric:** 0.45 x phoneme-sequence normalized edit similarity + 0.25 x stress-pattern per-syllable accuracy + 0.20 x **worst-stratum score** (min across native and held-out loanword strata — a loanword stratum whose vocabulary is *held out entirely* from the training lexicon, testing whether the model induced the stratum's rule from graphotactic cues rather than memorizing words) + 0.10 x **irregular-word subset accuracy** (a tagged subset of genuinely exceptional pronunciations that the regular rules cannot derive — an irreducible floor: even an oracle rule-follower misses these without memorization, and they're held out, so the score is capped). Min 0.0, max 1.0.

**Why hard for current agents:** invented language/phoneme inventory → no pretraining prior; a rule-lookup or single-rule model fails the held-out loanword stratum (different mapping) and the irregular subset (non-derivable); only a model that induces stratum from graphotactics and generalizes the phonological rule succeeds. The irregular subset guarantees oracle < 1.0.

**Why humans/experts can still improve it:** phonologists/TTS engineers can add graphotactic stratum-detection features and syllabification-aware decoding, lifting the worst-stratum and stress terms beyond a naive char-seq2seq.

**Shortcuts killed:** grapheme-copy baseline (phonemes differ from graphemes by construction), single-global-rule G2P (fails the loanword stratum), stratum-name lookup (not provided at test), word-length/majority-stress heuristics, and dictionary-memorization (fails the held-out stratum and held-out irregular words).

**Data source & license:** fully synthetic (Option A). A generator defines the constructed phonology, two+ strata with distinct G2P+stress rules and distinct graphotactics, a small set of logged irregular exceptions, and emits the corpus + lexicon. CC0 1.0.

**Dataset scale:** ~12MB unlabeled corpus; 15,000 train / 3,500 test lexicon entries. A few tens of MB.

**Split plan:** grouped by `stratum` (native + loanword strata) with one loanword stratum's vocabulary held out entirely; irregular-word subset held out and scored as its own hidden group.

**Schema:** public = word_id, grapheme_word (test); corpus.txt + lexicon.csv (train). Train-only = phoneme_sequence, stress_pattern, stratum (train-only, hidden at test). Hidden grader groups = stratum (worst-group, held-out loanword hardest) and irregular-word subset. Submission = word_id, phoneme_sequence, stress_pattern.

**Baseline plan:** `_analyze.py` reports grapheme-copy baseline, single-global-rule G2P (fit on native only, fails loanword stratum), majority-stress baseline, nearest-neighbor-by-surface lexicon lookup (fails held-out stratum + irregulars), and a from-scratch char-seq2seq skill ladder showing training helps.

**Nearest-neighbor novelty note:** neighbors are G2P research (CMUdict-based G2P, SIGMORPHON G2P shared tasks) and the repo's own "Synthetic Morphological Inflection" (inflection, not G2P+stress) and Sanskrit morphology (morpheme segmentation, not phonemic transduction). Delta: G2P + *stress* + a *loanword-stratum holdout* + an *irregular-word irreducible cap*, on a wholly constructed language, is a distinct contract from inflection or segmentation. Low external collision.
**Opus self-assessment of novelty risk: LOW-MODERATE.** SIGMORPHON G2P is a named shared-task family, so name it explicitly; the loanword-stratum-holdout + irregular-cap combination on a constructed language is the differentiator, and it does not collide with the repo's existing morphology entries (different task). Slight proximity to the existing Synthetic Morphological Inflection engine means the framing must foreground *phonemic transduction + stress*, not morphology.

**Suggested platform metadata:** Difficulty Medium; GPU A10G.
